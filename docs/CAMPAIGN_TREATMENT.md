## The length we are designing for

The target is **45–55 hours to complete the main story**, **70–90 hours for a first playthrough that includes a meaningful selection of optional adventures**, and **100–120 hours to experience essentially all authored content**. Use 50, 80, and 110 hours as planning centers, not advertised completion claims. New Game+, repeated clears, idle time, and grinding do not count toward these targets.

The reference is the scale of a substantial modern Final Fantasy or Zelda adventure, rather than a mathematical average of two franchises with very different entries. Firsthand estimates for Final Fantasy XVI put a normal story-focused run around 40 hours and a thorough first run around 60–80; Nintendo Life estimates Tears of the Kingdom's story at 50–60 hours. Square Enix described Rebirth as offering nearly 100 hours of adventure, which is a content-scope statement, not a measured player average.

Sources consulted November 4, 2026:

- [PowerPyx's Final Fantasy XVI playtime breakdown](https://www.powerpyx.com/how-long-is-final-fantasy-16-xvi-story-length/).
- [Nintendo Life's Tears of the Kingdom playtime guide](https://www.nintendolife.com/guides/zelda-tears-of-the-kingdom-how-long-does-it-take-to-beat).
- [Square Enix's Rebirth announcement and director's scope statement](https://press.de.square-enix.com/SQUARE-ENIX-ANNOUNCES-FINAL-FANTASY-VII-REBIRTH-WILL-LAUNCH-ON-FEBRUAR).

Our targets are a design judgment informed by those references. They are not a forecast derived from the prototype's current playtime. They require substantial new gameplay, world, encounter, and narrative production.

## What the first draft was missing

The premise supplied a strong opening and destination, but moved too quickly from discovering the fraud to assembling a resistance and defeating Caedran. The company needs time to misunderstand the problem, form relationships, win something important, make a costly mistake, and learn how to build a different future.

The expanded dramatic argument is: **being able to defeat a tyrant does not yet mean being able to replace what people depend on him for.**

Caedran's protection racket remains the central crime. The deeper complication is that the same network also powers water pumps, medical equipment, and safe trade routes. Breaking it carelessly can hurt the people the company wants to free. This supports discoveries, dungeons, engineering problems, political disagreements, and character growth without introducing a second mastermind or extending the ending through another hidden continent.

The campaign therefore has three changing goals:

1. Survive the betrayal and find Hearthmere's missing people.
2. Expose and defeat the occupation, then discover why military victories alone are insufficient.
3. Help the settlements sustain themselves, break the binding system, and remove Caedran permanently.

Across these external goals, the required story must develop the three heroes' bond into a found family. They begin as strangers forced together by the opening crisis. Individual arcs and supporting communities contribute to that central relationship. A proposed progression from there is reluctant cooperation, earned trust, a serious strain after a major setback, and conscious recommitment. Exact turning points must work across the eventual branches. Optional personal stories deepen the bond rather than supply its only meaningful development.

The selected hero's name and appearance are customizable, while their authored personality and history remain. Canonical names in this treatment identify class/story roles; preserve the chosen character's history and relationships across personalization. Scenes and awakened forms must represent the customized hero consistently. Generated voices or expressive Sims-like vocalizations are acceptable, and dialogue/story presentation needs vocal presence. Exact coverage and vocal style remain open.

The user requires different missions, alliances, final routes, and endings driven by player choices. This treatment currently describes one proposed route, not an approved event sequence regardless of player action. Revisit its compulsory refusal, alliances, rescues, and ending as part of the branch design. Choices cannot be limited to flavor or local epilogue references. Some decisions impose unavoidable permanent tradeoffs involving supporting people or places. The trio survives every branch. Reserve explicit warnings for the largest irreversible commitments; ordinary decisions need understandable dialogue/context. Exact branch content, factions, endings, shared events, and warning thresholds remain to be designed. The [narrative and cinematic brief](NARRATIVE_PRESENTATION.md) collects these requirements.

Caedran remains the enemy in every route. Branching alliances are among resisting groups, not a route joining his cause. Endings must range from hopeful to bittersweet to genuinely dark based on the choices, while preserving the trio. The resistance factions, their conflicts, commitment points, and exact outcomes are still being designed.

The user has delegated the resistance disagreements. [The adopted resistance design](RESISTANCE_DESIGN.md) establishes the Hearth Compact (civilian protection and local independence), the Free Standard (coordinated military victory), and the Severance Circle (permanent dismantling of coercive infrastructure). Alliances can shift through much of the game, with the largest commitments late. Earlier permanent consequences persist. Concrete missions, final approaches, and endings remain to be authored.

## The people who carry the longer story

Retain the main cast and motivations from the foundation. Add the following recurring people so the frontier can disagree with itself without every dispute being mediated by the same three NPCs.

**Rowan Venn**, Mara's younger brother, repairs pumps and farming equipment. He is rescued during the first half, not held hostage until the finale. His recovery is uneven, but he remains capable of work, humor, anger, and disagreement. His experience helps the company understand binding; he is never merely a source of experimental data.

**Nessa Fen**, a Reedwater ferry captain, runs a route that carries fish, medicine, prisoners, and information. She initially cooperates with occupation authorities to keep families supplied. Helping her should expose a real tradeoff between immediate relief and longer-term independence.

**Joren Pell**, Red Quarry's elected shift foreman, wants workers out of forced production. He also knows that destroying their only industry would leave their families hungry. He becomes an ally who challenges plans that look heroic from outside the settlement.

**Captain Ysra Tal**, the officer who takes the prisoners at Hearthmere, commands Caedran's mobile hunters. She is efficient, controlled, and personally committed to the belief that a disciplined occupation saves more lives than rebellion. Her early retreats are military withdrawals with surviving troops, not repeated death escapes. Her pursuit has a final resolution in the Hollow Arsenal.

**Commander Othric**, Brynhildr's former superior, commands the ridge regiment. He remembers Caedran's genuine defense of the frontier and believes dissent dishonors the dead. He is the human face of the oath Brynhildr must reject.

**Professor Dain Corven**, Celestia's former teacher, designed a temporal synchronization method now used to bind armored troops. He argues that collective safety justifies removing an individual's capacity to disobey. He and Celestia can understand the same mathematics and reach opposing moral conclusions.

**Lio**, Akatsuki's former partner, scouts for the independent Vesper Company. Their hunters take contracts from whoever keeps them alive. Lio offers a credible route out of the March, later becoming proof that self-preservation and loyalty need not remain enemies. This is a friendship with history, not a compulsory romance.

Introduce these people through situations in which their competence matters. Do not stop the opening to deliver seven biographies. Keep ordinary residents recurring too: the cook who learns the party's preferences, the apprentice whose workshop changes, and the children who copy the company's moves badly.

## I. The Town That Took Us In

The opening crisis around Hearthmere's royal shipment brings three strangers together. The earlier established-company premise is superseded. Develop each hero's individual reason for being present and a playable introduction appropriate to whichever one the player selected. These specifics remain proposals to write; do not assume shared employment, a joint debt, or prior friendship. Early exchanges should reveal contrasting instincts, with trust earned through their first acts of cooperation.

Hearthmere has prepared a small welcome for the shipment because its promised refined metal will repair failing defenses. Give the player a short period inside a living town before the attack: meet Mara and Rowan, see the damaged pump, share food, and learn where people gather. This makes the subsequent destruction legible.

When the cargo opens, the assault crosses places the player has just visited. Fighting protects movement and evacuation: hold a lane while civilians cross, break the beast pinning the forge door, choose an approach around a burning cart. The central rescue succeeds without requiring every monster to die.

Tal's soldiers arrive under the guise of relief. Rusk discovers their orders and refuses to execute witnesses. The three heroes help him drive them back, but Tal withdraws with prisoners through the old mill road. In this proposed route, Mara initially suspects the newcomers after the attack. The exact suspicion should follow their eventual individual introductions rather than assume they all escorted the shipment. They stay when fleeing would be easier, beginning the cooperation that will become a company.

The first substantial expedition follows that road to **Old Harth Mill**, a grain works converted into a staging site. The captives have already been transferred; the player instead frees a wounded survivor and finds the destination of their transport. This is one consequential missed interception, not a pattern of arriving too late all game. The mill's remaining handlers release a plated burrower through the foundations rather than surrender.

Back in Hearthmere, testimony contradicts the official warrant. The town decides to shelter the company. Mara gives it an abandoned riverside house to use, with a leaking roof and enough room for three beds. Home is established through an act of trust before it becomes a restoration project.

**Change by the end:** the company has somewhere to return, a named pursuer, and a real lead. The mill road remains open and the rescued survivor lives in town.

## II. The Country Beyond the Road

The company leaves the main caravan road and discovers that the March is not uniformly ruined. Some occupied villages have functioning wells and protected markets. Some free villages cannot get medicine through. Caedran's propaganda works because it exploits these differences.

At Mossbridge, woodland hunters dispute whether to shelter outsiders. A monster has diverted prey and driven smaller predators into farms. Investigating its territory leads beneath a failed extraction works into the **Rootvault**, where living roots have grown around abandoned machinery. The company finds Sera protecting workers who were left behind when she fled the Covenant.

Sera helps explain the shipment and the resonance in the heroes' weapons. She also confesses that she accepted reassuring reports about missing research subjects. She knew enough to ask questions and chose professional comfort. Saving her does not settle that responsibility.

The Rootvault's ecological crisis is resolved by redirecting the raw discharge feeding it. Its apex creature remains a real threat requiring a fight, while the underlying lesson is that killing beasts alone does not stop new ones gathering. The company acquires a shared resonance lens that reveals active currents in machinery and disturbed terrain. It adds ways to investigate and navigate; it does not fill the map with compulsory collectibles.

Lio recognizes Akatsuki at a roadside market. Vesper has received the company's bounty but has not accepted it. Lio offers passage out of the March, with a departure that waits for an explicit decision rather than a hidden timer. Akatsuki says she is considering it; she also makes sure the others have enough supplies before she would leave.

Nessa and Joren provide independent leads into the river prison network and quarry shipments. The company can pursue either first, switching between them. Essential evidence can be learned from more than one contact, so missing an early conversation cannot break progression.

**Change by the end:** the player understands the local threat but has seen why exposing it will not automatically win public support. Sera has a workshop in Hearthmere and a reason to work with people she once ignored.

## III. Two Kinds of Prison

This movement contains two regional stories. Their order is flexible and their dialogue accounts for what the company already knows.

**Reedwater: the prison that looks like safety.** Nessa's crossing feeds Lowbank, a fishing settlement whose defenses genuinely hold the Bloom back. The company finds a prison infirmary hidden inside the reservoir works. Some patients volunteered for armor after losing family to creatures. Others were bound after refusing occupation orders.

The **Drowned Sluice** is a working water system as well as a prison. Its pumps keep parts of Lowbank dry. The company must establish a safe flow through the machinery before opening the cells. Nessa and Rowan's fellow mechanics can operate the restored works while the company fights below. The player sees a practical alternative to destroying everything.

Rowan is recovered here. He can talk, recognize the company, and help, but a command pulse still disrupts his movement. Removing his armor requires a careful disconnection performed with his consent. During the escape, an older bound soldier chooses to cover the prisoners' retreat and is saved if the company breaks the command conduit during the encounter. This introduces liberation as an action the player can perform, not a line spoken after a boss dies.

**Red Quarry: the prison that calls itself work.** Joren is preparing a stoppage at Cinderwash, but families depend on its wages and stockpiled heat cells. Tal threatens to classify the settlement as a Bloom outbreak if production falters. The company follows loading marks to a beast convoy that can be intercepted on the road in either regional order.

The **Red Furnace** expedition moves from labor floors into the beast-conditioning chambers beneath them. Its overseer fights while shifting molten channels and opening selected pens, making the industrial space part of the encounter. Winning allows workers to preserve the civilian foundry while closing the binding line. Joren organizes relief rather than immediately becoming a resistance soldier.

Completion of both stories supplies public witnesses, a surviving production record, and a real interruption to Caedran's operations. Rowan returns home during this movement and remains in the cast. Hearthmere's reunion is an earned pause: the company cooks badly, the siblings argue, and nobody has to explain the main conspiracy over dinner.

**Change by the end:** the company can prove the racket and has liberated people who matter. It has also learned that civilian infrastructure and military control are entangled.

## IV. The Victory We Wanted

Nessa arranges a meeting of settlement representatives at an abandoned observatory chapel. Each arrives with a different condition: transport, civilian control of the foundry, food guarantees, or amnesty for conscripts. Rusk is confronted by a family displaced under orders he enforced. He admits responsibility without asking the meeting to forgive him before it can proceed.

D122 confirms a first face-to-face, tense parley around the campaign midpoint after the trio knows the attacks are manufactured. Its exact purpose, terms, placement relative to liberation, and outcome still need review; the following causeway scene is an older proposal. Caedran requests a truce before the representatives commit. The company meets him on a public causeway with witnesses from both sides. He acknowledges unauthorized attacks while blaming excesses on officers, offers material relief, and proposes that Sera rebuild his system with safeguards. The player can question him and hear specific answers. Caedran remains the antagonist in every route; this scene must not open a full campaign joining his cause. How the company responds and how resisting factions react should be authored within the branching structure. Exact negotiations and resulting missions remain open.

After the truce, Corven offers Celestia access to the **Meridian Chapel**, where his synchronization research began. He insists she can prove the Crown safe if she stops treating the work as politically contaminated. Inside, damaged temporal regulators let the player alter the sequence of moving platforms, barriers, and hostile machinery. The history reveals that command capacity was deliberately added to civilian protection research.

Celestia rejects Corven's solution, but takes part of the mechanism as a basis for Sera's counter-signal. The company next strikes the **Hollow Arsenal**, where Tal is preparing a mass armor deployment. This is the campaign's first operation planned with several settlements. The player infiltrates its supply approach, frees an internal work crew, and fights through assembly galleries as allies seize exits.

Tal confronts the company at the dispatch platform. Freed subjects contradict her belief that binding is merciful. She refuses surrender and dies defending the armor deployment. Her pursuit ends here. The company captures the arsenal and its apparent victories seem to validate the new coalition.

Corven escapes the chapel and tells Caedran exactly what the company took. The antagonist learns from an identifiable surviving participant; he is not omniscient. Sera believes her prototype can protect Hearthmere during the retaliation the company now expects.

**Change by the end:** the company is no longer simply being hunted. It has won a public victory and thinks it has found a technical answer to Caedran's power.

## V. Hearthmere Burns

> Revision status: the user selected civilian-led defense versus emergency military command as the replacement siege model in [the branching campaign](BRANCHING_CAMPAIGN.md). The older sequence below supplies staging ideas only; forced Sera capture, Mara death, and regiment-transfer outcomes are not active requirements. The new draft supplies civilian-witness or military-record approaches to Whitewake, with Sera continuing her research outside the prison. Mandate terms and particular costs remain under review.

The warning arrives through a captured dispatch and visible troop movement. The player chooses when to begin the defense after a clear preparation conversation. Optional exploration remains available before that commitment.

The opening defense rewards familiarity with the approaches, repaired town structures, and allies already helped. A viable default plan exists. Optional preparation changes available tactics and civilian damage, not whether a player who skipped side stories is allowed to continue.

Caedran does not send another expendable captain. He comes to recover the stolen mechanism, using Corven's knowledge to turn the prototype's synchronized signal into a precise attack on the town's defenses. The failure exposes the flaw in Celestia and Sera's approach: they modified a system that still depended on one controlling source.

The company wins local fights and evacuates the threatened district, but cannot hold everything. The enemy captures the riverside workshop and Sera, and destroys the mill crossing. The company prevents a total massacre by severing power, then helping people escape through the waterworks it repaired earlier. Rowan guides families through a route his trade makes him qualified to understand.

In this proposed route, the town survives in a scarred, playable state. Permanent character losses should follow authored situations and consequential decisions rather than arbitrary random assignment. Some future branch choices can make losses unavoidable; no particular victim or lost settlement is selected yet. The riverside home is damaged; possessions and equipment are not confiscated to manufacture urgency. Optional improvements remain represented in who escaped and what still functions.

The next stretch slows down without becoming a list of recovery errands. Mara argues that more heroic attacks may simply bring more retaliation. Rusk offers to surrender himself and is refused because execution would solve nothing. Celestia admits she wanted to prove her mastery as much as help. Brynhildr realizes that holding ground at any cost nearly repeated her old commander's mistakes. Akatsuki stays after Lio provides a genuinely usable escape plan.

The company chooses a more difficult objective: make the settlements capable of surviving the Crown's destruction, then remove Caedran. Sera's rescue is part of that task, but the party can begin with her surviving notes and local expertise. Nobody waits helplessly for one scientist to return.

**Change by the end:** the resistance has suffered a real setback. Its purpose has matured. Home retains visible scars instead of being reset or discarded for a new hub.

## VI. A Country That Can Stand

Two connected efforts rebuild the coalition: recovering the people and knowledge taken to the ridge, and developing distributed protection that settlements can control locally. They can overlap.

At Stonewake, the company encounters Othric's regiment. Its soldiers once protected these passes voluntarily. Othric now accepts binding as a way to ensure they never break under pressure. Brynhildr meets former comrades, hears why they stayed, and helps deserters who are terrified of being recognized.

The **Whitewake Monastery** has become a convalescent station and research prison. Sera is kept alive to finish Corven's work. She has quietly collaborated with prisoners and staff, leaving a feasible rescue route rather than passively awaiting a key. Akatsuki and Lio help identify ways in; every player class can use the eventual approach. The dungeon's shelters and exposed galleries make rescue, visibility, and weather relevant alongside combat.

Othric fights to stop the prisoners leaving. Brynhildr rejects his oath openly. The company disables his armor's binding feed and defeats him; he survives to face the people he harmed. His regiment's later support is contingent on freeing its soldiers and keeping a public promise of amnesty, rather than automatically becoming a reward for beating its commander.

Sera returns with knowledge of how Caedran coordinates the network. Corven tried to make the Crown's authority universal, but field failures forced workers to improvise local protections. The answer exists partly in practical knowledge dismissed by his academy.

The second major expedition reaches **the First Fall**, the crater beneath the northeast ridge. The outer approaches have always been reachable, while the newly stabilized machinery permits a safe descent into its core. No new region is teleported into existence. The crater reveals a complex alien ecology, not a sleeping god that excuses Caedran or replaces him.

Within the crater, resonance travels through multiple interacting growths. The player learns to redirect a current without dominating the whole structure. The company defeats an apex organism whose territory encloses a stable sample chamber. Sera, Rowan, Joren's technicians, and Nessa's pump operators use this discovery to build a local regulator that settlements can maintain themselves.

The first public trial takes place at Lowbank. The player helps protect the site while its operators switch from Covenant control to independent power. This is a new situation in a familiar place: people who once opposed rebellion can see an alternative. It is not a forced re-clear of the Drowned Sluice.

Hearthmere then holds its first small market since the siege. Food and supplies are limited, and damaged homes remain visible. Allies contribute what they can. A brief shared meal and a restrained joke about the company's house repairs provide rare relief. This quieter sequence develops the trio's bond before the final conflict without turning recovery into a carefree festival.

**Change by the end:** the campaign has a workable answer to civilian dependency. Sera is free, the party has changed how it works, and the coalition can offer more than promises.

## VII. The Marshal's Peace

Caedran offers separate settlements favorable surrender terms before the independent regulators spread. He returns prisoners, cancels selected debts, and offers to lift trade restrictions. He is trying to fracture the alliance with tangible benefits, not asking everyone to forget an obvious massacre.

Nessa considers a deal when a relief shipment is held at a checkpoint. Joren refuses to send workers into a war without civilian control of their supplies. Ridge deserters fear that the coalition's promises will evaporate after victory. The company must settle these conflicts through bounded, authored operations and public commitments. There is no requirement to complete every village story or fill a reputation bar.

Three military fronts define the advance. Allied crews secure the civilian foundry while the player breaks an outbound beast operation. Freed ridge soldiers help open a pass through their former fortifications. At the **Signal Spine**, the company climbs an integrated chain of relays and maintenance structures to cut long-range Crown control while preserving the independent networks below.

Each front has a complete dramatic outcome and a distinct approach. Their order can vary once the technical preparation exists. Earlier achievements change starting conditions. Destroying a convoy early stays meaningful; the game does not respawn it to satisfy a later journal step.

Corven faces Celestia at the Spine. He proposes using the coming victory to place a benevolent operator in the Crown. When she refuses, he uses the synchronized defense system against the party. Defeating him preserves his research as public evidence while ending his command of the relays. He can survive under guard; his knowledge does not earn immunity.

Caedran responds by withdrawing surviving bound forces to Crownscar and abandoning the frontier protections he claimed were his life's work. Settlements using the independent regulators hold. The player sees his argument fail through concrete events, not only a triumphant speech.

The coalition gathers near Hearthmere. Its agreement gives settlements authority over their own defenses and puts prisoners under shared civilian supervision. Rusk volunteers to submit to testimony after the fighting. This is limited, imperfect cooperation among people with memories and disagreements, not instantaneous national unity.

**Change by the end:** the route to Crownscar is open, civilian survival no longer depends on its master, and the people taking part have a reason to trust one another.

## VIII. The Crown Comes Down

The player can finish optional business before explicitly committing to the assault. Exploration afterward remains possible; the warning describes the upcoming sustained finale rather than claiming the entire game ends permanently.

The approach crosses the same ridge landmarks visible early in the game. Allies conduct their own operations so the company is not required to fight every soldier between the valley and the keep. The player moves through an outer siege, prisoner yards, foundry terraces, and the Crown's command halls. Rest and narrative pauses separate sustained sequences; saving is not restricted to those points. Exact active-battle and cinematic resume rules remain to be designed.

The company carries a short-range liberation device. Its use interrupts particular command feeds in encounters; it does not solve every fight at once. Joren's crews and freed soldiers operate supporting emitters. All required support has a baseline version from the main path, with optional stories improving it.

Caedran attempts a final division: he offers the company control of the network once he dies, claiming somebody must make the difficult decisions. The heroes' answer is demonstrated through their plan. They already built the means to destroy it without sacrificing the settlements.

The final confrontation has three distinct changes in behavior. Caedran first coordinates honor guards and beasts. With his support disabled, he fights directly as the commander who once held the frontier pass. When losing, he fuses the Crown into his armor and becomes a formidable but increasingly exposed living command source. His tactics grow more controlling as his actual control collapses.

The company wins, destroys the Crown, and helps survivors out through routes opened during the assault. The fortress's industrial structures fail locally; the world outside is not erased in a universal explosion. Allies complete the relay shutdown. Caedran is definitively defeated.

The ending visits the settlements through a playable return and selected short scenes. Rowan repairs something ordinary without hearing an order in his head. Othric's surviving soldiers take off armor in public. Corven and Rusk face different forms of accountability for different actions. Sera teaches rather than presides. Mara and the company share a meal in the square that the player knew before it burned.

The three heroes decide to keep traveling together. Their reasons have changed: protecting people rather than preserving an oath; sharing knowledge rather than owning the answer; staying because attachment is worth the risk. The active player hero participates in these resolutions rather than watching two companions do all the growing.

## Ten major dungeons that earn their place

Target roughly 75–120 minutes for most first main-path visits, with smaller opening works and a longer, segmented finale. These visits are included in the 50-hour main-story budget and can span multiple 30–60-minute sessions. The user requires stopping almost anywhere and resuming the same situation; dungeon length is not a requirement for uninterrupted play. Each location needs an authored layout, a mechanical idea introduced safely and then combined under pressure, a recognizable climax, and a lasting result.

| Dungeon | Central play idea | Story result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Old Harth Mill | Grain lifts, rotating drive shafts, branching loading routes, a burrower breaking floors | Establishes the prison route and frees a surviving witness. |
| Rootvault | Redirect living currents through root-covered machinery; track creature territory | Finds Sera and stops the local source of repeated Bloom incursions. |
| Drowned Sluice | Change water levels while preserving a safe civilian flow; connect prisoner escape routes | Rescues Rowan and exposes the armor trials. |
| Red Furnace | Move heat and machinery between civilian and military lines; fight around active beast pens | Keeps the useful foundry while ending its binding production. |
| Meridian Chapel | Change the timing of regulators, platforms, and barriers; combine sequences in combat | Exposes Corven's contribution and supplies the flawed prototype mechanism. |
| Hollow Arsenal | Intervene in a working deployment; choose paths through assembly galleries and occupied workrooms | Ends Tal's pursuit and gives the resistance its first major victory. |
| Whitewake Monastery | Link sheltered routes across exposed galleries; move prisoners through occupied spaces | Frees Sera and confronts Othric's doctrine. |
| The First Fall | Read an alien ecosystem and redirect interacting currents rather than shut everything down | Provides the basis for independent protection and a different understanding of the Bloom. |
| Signal Spine | Climb and reconnect maintenance routes while selectively cutting enemy signals | Ends Corven's control and isolates the Crown. |
| Crownscar | Combine established traversal and liberation systems in a siege with multiple stages | Defeats Caedran and brings prisoners and allies out alive. |

Reuse underlying systems, but vary the spatial problems and consequences. Ten palette swaps of one corridor do not satisfy this inventory. Timed inscription quizzes are not a substitute for these dungeon mechanics.

## Three personal stories with room to breathe

Essential character beats are already in the main campaign. Each hero additionally has an optional approximately three-hour personal storyline, split across several substantial encounters and quiet scenes. Nine hours total is the planning allowance for all three; shared main-story scenes are not counted again here. They remain available when that hero is the player character.

**Brynhildr: The Names on the Standard.** She finds survivors of a retreat her old regiment describes as cowardice. Their leader actually abandoned a strategic position to evacuate civilians. She first helps them conceal their identities, then discovers one has been collecting military pensions for dead friends to feed surviving families. Protecting them does not mean endorsing every deception. The climax defends a civilian hearing from officers attempting to destroy its witnesses. The resolution creates a place for veterans outside Caedran's ideal of endless service, with a combat reward built around guarding others rather than simply dealing more damage.

**Celestia: A Lesson Without a Master.** An apprentice has copied a dangerous academy technique to keep a failing clinic operating. Celestia initially tries to confiscate it, then recognizes her teacher's habit of treating less qualified people as obstacles. Working with patients and a local mechanic, she helps develop a safer method and confronts an academy retrieval team claiming ownership of it. ## How we determine whether it is actually a full-length game

The targets remain unverified until the game is playable. First build a complete Hearthmere-and-mill slice with exploration, town life, combat, an authored dungeon, a boss, and persistent aftermath. Include players unfamiliar with the map and record active play, dialogue, navigation, combat, retries, and idle time separately. Report wall-clock completion too, with its inclusion rules.

Then build a complete regional arc containing a settlement, a main dungeon, a saga, and an optional discovery. This tests whether the proposed variety and revisit structure work beyond the opening. Use the observed production effort and playtime to revise the remaining inventory. One strong opening cannot establish a 50-hour game's pacing or cost.

For the eventual full campaign, use a mixed group of first-time players on normal difficulty, with both story-focused and exploratory intentions. Report median and spread, not a single fastest run. Track where players get lost, repeat low-value combat, skim scenes, abandon optional stories, or feel compelled to grind. Diagnose those stretches before treating their duration as useful playtime.

If the main path is short, expand underdeveloped conflicts with new decisions, authored spaces, or encounter ideas. If it drags, cut redundant scenes and travel even when that lowers the hour count. Never claim the reference length until full-game first-run evidence supports it.
