The underlying faction priorities here are delegated design; the newer draft's particular scenes and outcome conditions are proposals for review.

## The central disagreement

All three groups oppose Caedran. They disagree about what must take priority when they cannot protect everyone, defeat him quickly, and permanently remove his means of control at the same time.

The three priorities are **protect the people living here now**, **win the war before more people are bound**, and **make sure no successor can inherit the machinery of coercion**. These priorities intersect with civilian independence, military coordination, and the future of star-metal technology. No group is simply indifferent to civilian life.

## The Hearth Compact

An alliance of settlement representatives, local defenders, ferrymen, growers, and craftspeople. It prioritizes keeping people housed, fed, free, and able to govern their communities.

It favors evacuations, relief routes, local defenses, and adapting civilian star-metal systems to work independently. Its objection to a single resistance command is practical: a town should not lose the ability to refuse another distant leader's demand for sacrifice.

Its strength is legitimacy and people who know how the frontier actually functions. Its weakness is slow agreement and a tendency to protect familiar communities while people elsewhere remain captive. Saving a town today can consume resources needed to stop the next prison transport.

Existing characters such as Mara, Nessa, and Joren offer useful connections to this outlook. Their precise membership and loyalties are proposals, not fixed solely by their occupations.

## The Free Standard

A resistance force joining deserters, veteran defenders, independent hunters, and volunteers. It prioritizes defeating Caedran's military capacity quickly enough to prevent further binding and retaliation.

It favors coordinated offensives, captured military supplies, secure transport, and a unified operational command. It rejects enslaving soldiers. It wants to capture useful infrastructure and use its logistical and defensive functions during the war, with the details of dismantling it to follow victory.

Its strength is decisive action and the ability to confront organized opposition. Its weakness is accepting civilian risk in pursuit of strategic advantage, then asking the public to trust that emergency command will eventually give up power. Some survivors distrust it because former Covenant personnel are among its most capable members.

Rusk and existing deserter stories provide useful entry points. His accountability must remain real; a new banner does not erase his history.

## The Severance Circle

A movement of escaped subjects, research defectors, victims' families, and specialists who understand how command technology can be rebuilt. It prioritizes permanently destroying the capacity to bind people or direct mass attacks through a centralized signal.

It favors dismantling command relays, destroying the enabling research, and removing components that would let another ruler recreate the Crown. It distinguishes ordinary local star-metal tools from the network that makes coercion possible. It does not demand indiscriminate destruction of every metal tool, the heroes' powers, or civilian life.

Its strength is confronting the long-term danger of inheriting Caedran's system. Its weakness is acting before communities have adequate replacements for interconnected infrastructure. Ending a command link can also strand a clinic, a pump system, or prisoners whose safe release depends on careful disconnection.

The movement has expertise and legitimate grievances, not supernatural certainty. It can underestimate the work required to keep people alive after a decisive act of destruction.

## How choices become missions

These are authoring directions to develop, not approved quests:

| Situation | Hearth Compact priority | Free Standard priority | Severance Circle priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A retreat exposes civilians while bound troops move toward a new deployment | Secure an evacuation and relief corridor | Strike the deployment before it reaches another settlement | Destroy the transfer's command apparatus before it can be reused |
| A captured industrial site supports civilians and military production | Keep local services running under civilian control | Hold its supply capacity for the campaign | Remove the coercive production chain permanently |
| An approach to Crownscar becomes available | Establish a way to protect and extract people along it | Use the opening before the defenders reorganize | Reach the source of command and prevent its capture by any successor |

All three priorities can sometimes align. At major authored decisions, limited capacity or incompatible plans can make a perfect outcome impossible. Do not manufacture a tragedy at every routine errand or add a hidden completionist solution to every tradeoff.

## Alliance timing and boundaries

Alliances can shift through much of the game, with the largest route commitments late (D54). The player should understand groups through missions, local outcomes, disagreements, and relationships before those commitments.

Changing alliances changes future opportunities, trust, and planning; it does not undo permanent losses. Exact thresholds, mission exclusivity, commitment points, and any reputation implementation remain open. A single numerical reputation bar is not yet selected.

The selected siege authority fork can give the military emergency powers with no guaranteed end date (D90–D91). Recovering civilian control requires a later persuasion/force conflict; changing allies or defeating Caedran does not automatically do it. The Free Standard can relinquish those powers and remain a useful military ally, or entrench them. Record the actual authority outcome rather than equating its initial command role with a predetermined dark ending.

Caedran remains the enemy across every route (D51). These are different responses to his rule, not a route to joining it. Distinct final missions, approaches, and endings are required (D47), but they are not specified by this faction brief.

## Ending principles

The ending range is hopeful, bittersweet, and genuinely dark (D52). Do not assign the Compact a guaranteed good ending, the Standard a neutral ending, and the Circle an evil ending. Each group's priorities can protect some things and endanger others; outcomes depend on actual choices and earlier consequences.

The core trio survives every ending (D15). Supporting lives and places can be permanently lost, including in unavoidable tradeoffs (D48). The family's survival does not guarantee the world is healed, the resistance remains united, or its institutions are just.

Explicit warnings are reserved for the largest irreversible commitments (D49). Ordinary choices must communicate through understandable circumstances and dialogue. Detailed warning conditions, save/reload behavior, and the final branch map remain open.
