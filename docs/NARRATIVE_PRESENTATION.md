# Narrative, performance, and cinematics — design brief

Interview draft, November 4, 2026. This brief collects confirmed presentation and narrative requirements and identifies open decisions. It does not implement cutscenes or approve new story branches. Read the [design decision record](DESIGN_DIRECTION.md) for original answers and status.

## Confirmed creative foundation

- Beautiful cinematic scenes are a core requirement (D05).
- Heavy, over-the-top anime influence shapes abilities, effects, and atmosphere (D06).
- References have distinct roles: Final Fantasy for huge boss encounters, Solo Leveling for epic fights, and The Last of Us for emotional impact (D08–D10).
- Strongly cel-shaded 3D uses bold forms and shadow shapes. Heroes receive major redesigns that preserve identities and class silhouettes (D22–D23).
- The selected hero's name and appearance are customizable while their authored personality and history remain (D44).
- The world is harsh and tense, with rare moments of warmth. The three heroes becoming a close found family is the emotional center (D13–D14).
- The trio begins as strangers forced together by the opening crisis; shared history as an existing company is superseded (D50).
- All three core heroes survive. Supporting losses follow the individually confirmed branch decisions, including D88, D94, and D112; D15 alone does not approve additional deaths.

## Voice and vocal presence

The user accepts procedurally generated voices, including Sims-like vocalizations, provided dialogue/story presentation has vocal presence rather than silence (D45). Traditional intelligible performance on every line is not required by this answer.

The exact mix of intelligible generated speech and expressive nonliteral sounds, and coverage of routine NPC conversations, remain open. If sounds are nonliteral, readable dialogue must communicate the actual meaning. Preserve character emotion and identity through delivery, facial animation, body language, pacing, and sound together.

The user has not selected a generation service, synthesis technique, language, spoken-name system, live generation, or pre-generated audio pipeline. Voice production is separate from story authorship: generated audio does not make the narrative or dialogue randomly improvised.

## Choice-driven narrative

Caedran's mother made love conditional on obedience and achievement; he still seeks approval and demands obedience from others (D117–D120). She is alive and present, bluntly belittling him: "you ain’t shit." He argues back but repeatedly returns for a kind word. Show both defiance and renewed pursuit of approval rather than passive submission or a fixed retaliation beat. Specific childhood events, her identity/influence, public/private setting, dialogue, and frequency remain open. The proposed contrast is conditional protection versus the trio's freely chosen trust. Keep the history personal and consequential without assuming a diagnosis, hidden mastermind, or an exculpatory final speech.

The opening includes roughly 15–20 minutes of playable town life before the crisis (D101). Use required-path interactions to establish Mara, Rowan, and a recognizable Hearthmere, so later losses do not depend on optional conversations. The [opening sequence draft](OPENING_HOUR.md) proposes concrete scenes. Preserve the harsh setting with rare warmth, initial strangers, and player control; this is not a long exposition cinematic or an approved profession tutorial.

The first major action sequence is survival, rescue, and escape from an overwhelming giant (D102). Establish its scale cinematically, then return to readable playable objectives. The player should know that escape is the goal; do not present a standard kill encounter and replace apparent success with a forced defeat. Smaller victories and rescued people give the grounded heroes agency without defeating the giant or prematurely using their endgame forms.

The same giant returns in Hearthmere's defense, and the trio kills it with a spectacular, earned finisher while protecting the same town and people (D103–D105). Use recognizable movement, town geography, and visual identity to connect the encounters, then demonstrate new player capabilities and earned cooperation through playable action. Both siege authority approaches retain this payoff. Exact phases, available abilities, and choreography remain open. The finisher must not replace the player's role in earning victory or reset earlier personal and political consequences. The creature stays dead in the continuing world.

Hearthmere is occupied between the opening and that defense (D106). Covert resident cooperation prepares a covert liberation that dismantles the garrison's control and removes its leadership before a full response (D107); do not play older open-refuge scenes unchanged. Show a living community under pressure, then a meaningful change when public life resumes. The trio earns a home before having to defend it. Exposure before leadership is isolated turns liberation into harder combat with changed objectives, without automatically failing the operation (D108). Exact occupation scenes, escalation tasks, timing, and leadership fate remain proposals. All three selected heroes need viable playable roles, with automatic companion action respecting the plan's dependencies.

The occupation commander sincerely believes coercive military control protects the town, and is capable at the job (D109). They believe the official account and do not know the attacks are manufactured; credible evidence can challenge their loyalty (D110). Show conviction through useful action and its human costs rather than a long explanatory monologue. Specific proof and reaction scenes remain proposals; D111–D112 establish the possible conditional alliance and fatal unconvinced defeat. Ignorance of the conspiracy does not erase responsibility for occupation policies. Do not replace this characterization with a last-minute sadism reveal or let a persuasion line automatically resolve trust and accountability.

A long-term alliance can be earned after relinquishing occupation authority, facing accountability, and building trust through later actions (D111). Stage those changes in behavior and relationships rather than resolving them in one conversion cinematic. Specific eligibility, actions, and living-branch scenes remain proposals. The core trio remains the party; a former enemy's help does not require instant acceptance by every resident or undo earlier personal consequences.

All three heroes first awaken during Hearthmere's giant rematch, as its turning point (D113–D114). Complete earned mutual trust supplies the emotional catalyst: they combine their learned mastery instead of fighting as individuals (D115). Express that through complementary commitments seeded in earlier required scenes, then reveal three distinct forms and return to meaningful awakened play before the killing finisher. Preserve prior mastery and meter/manual activation. Exact actions, dialogue, phase design, reveal order, duration, and handoff remain proposals. Both siege authority approaches include the event, with participants matching actual relationship states. The shared trust milestone leaves their later grief, responsibilities, and disagreements meaningful.

Their form reveals combine intimidating beauty, dark metal, fierce auras, and visibly dangerous power with distinct identities (D116): radiant spear champion, cosmic time mage, and shadow-wreathed assassin. Carry that tone through silhouette, expression, motion, and sound while keeping the customized hero recognizable and combat readable. Specific designs and effects remain proposals. Dangerous appearance does not imply the trio loses control, becomes hostile, or acquires a new gameplay penalty.

If defeated while unconvinced, the officer fights to the death, permanently ending that alliance opportunity (D112). Preserve the distinction between death in confrontation and an execution after surrender. Exact proof opportunities and lethal-resolution staging need authoring; a hidden dialogue trap or automatic death merely for triggering an alert is not the selected rule. Later scenes must respect who actually survived.

The user requires choices to matter and drive the narrative (D46), with different missions, alliances, final routes, and endings (D47). A shared finale with only altered epilogue lines is not the selected structure. Exact routes, factions, branch points, and endings remain to be authored.

The [branching campaign draft](BRANCHING_CAMPAIGN.md) develops consequential early/middle operations and persistent character/place facts around the confirmed three-route, nine-ending structure (D96). Each route has a distinct hopeful, bittersweet, and dark conclusion, with additional personal/world variations. Particular titles, conditions, and scenes need review and detailed authoring; they are not newly confirmed requirements simply because they are written.

The reservoir tradeoff is confirmed (D88). The rejected siege sacrifice split (D89) is replaced by civilian-led defense versus emergency military command, affecting the resistance's power afterward (D90). Specific scenes/costs and mandate terms are still proposals. Do not restore the forced Mara death, Sera capture, or regiment transfer. Whitewake now has proposed civilian-witness or military-record approaches. Later missions test the mandate and promises rather than repeatedly asking a fresh civilian-versus-military question.

Emergency powers have no guaranteed end date (D91). Persuasion or force to recover civilian control becomes a later conflict, not an automatic post-siege or post-Caedran scene. The branch draft proposes **The Orders That Remain** with relief-route action, a command confrontation, negotiated/forced transfer, and continued-authority consequences. These particulars remain proposals. Do not make Rusk a secret villain or a harmless bystander to orders his command enabled.

The Compact finale must allow both Sera's ward/rescue group and the allied rearguard to survive through preparation and playable action (D92). A failed rescue objective while the player survives opens an in-mission recovery opportunity; failing recovery can cause a permanent loss (D93). Communicate the threatened group and recovery action through the scene before resolving that loss. Remove the earlier guaranteed either/or sacrifice. Keep the reservoir's unavoidable loss and other prior consequences intact. The proposed bypass, withdrawal route, broadcast interruption, and evacuation stages still need encounter staging and detailed recovery conditions; success cannot be supplied solely by a cutscene in which the player watches their own hero act.

The Free Standard finale's unavoidable forge-district-versus-bound-conscripts tradeoff is confirmed (D94). Show the stakes before commitment and make both chosen plans playable. Evacuation preparation can save district residents, not the district and conscripts together. Reflect the actual loss in the return journey without treating either plan as an automatic verdict on military accountability. Exact participants, geometry, and scenes remain to be authored.

The Severance Circle's gradual-versus-immediate shutdown decision is confirmed (D95). Gradual shutdown protects dependent patients but allows the last command to cause allied troop losses. Immediate shutdown protects those troops, while earlier civilian preparation affects clinics and water systems. Communicate known dependencies before commitment and show preparation's actual effects in the world. Specific patient outcomes, loss severity, scenes, and ending conditions remain authoring proposals.

Future story design should make each major choice concrete: the situation and information available, the actual alternatives, what each changes, which characters act differently, which events or missions follow, and where consequences become visible. These are design criteria, not a selected branch catalog.

Mara's relationship after Rowan's death is a confirmed personal branch (D99): later choices can rebuild trust or lead to a permanent break. She understands why the reservoir choice was unavoidable; grief is not removed by either outcome. After a break she refuses direct cooperation, with changed missions/support and approaches through other allies (D100). She continues opposing Caedran, and the Compact remains available. Develop her own responsibilities, needs, and public role rather than reducing her to a forgiveness reward. Specific trust triggers and alternate operation scenes remain proposals in the branch draft. Keep this separate from the core trio's unbroken bond and from a simple faction-reputation gate.

The [story foundation](OPEN_WORLD_STORY.md) and [campaign treatment](CAMPAIGN_TREATMENT.md) describe an older proposed route. Their alliance sequence and ending must follow the newer branching structure where they conflict. Do not assume those events happen identically regardless of choice. Caedran remains the enemy; branching alliances are among the groups resisting him (D51). Do not add a full route joining his cause. The count is now three final routes and nine main endings (D96); exact shared content and branch scenes still need development.

The trio's survival remains a boundary in every branch. Their authored personalities also remain: player agency does not automatically turn the selected hero into a character without a history or consistent motivations.

The user delegated the resistance conflict. [The resistance design](RESISTANCE_DESIGN.md) establishes competing priorities of civilian protection, coordinated military victory, and permanent removal of coercive infrastructure. Alliances can shift through much of the game; the largest route commitments come late (D53–D54). Earlier permanent consequences are not reset by changing allies.

Endings span hopeful, bittersweet, and genuinely dark outcomes based on choices (D52), with three distinct conclusions per route (D96). World recovery and personal cost jointly determine hopeful versus bittersweet (D97). Personal consequences must be able to change the principal ending, not only the memorial lines inside it. Show both the condition of communities and the actual relationships, losses, and prospects of the people who remain. The core trio survives and retains its found-family bond in all of them (D98), facing consequences together even in the darkest outcome. Show that bond through care and commitment; do not conclude with permanent estrangement or unresolved mistrust among the three. Losses, supporting relationships, homes, and obligations can still supply profound personal costs. Exact outcome combinations, supporting reactions, and final scenes need authoring. Do not substitute a purely cosmetic tonal variation for the required distinct final routes and endings.

### Permanent tradeoffs and communication

Some decisions have no perfect answer: saving one supporting person or place can mean permanently losing another (D48). Good preparation is not a universal route to saving everyone. The branching campaign now records individually confirmed losses and preparation-dependent successes; other named losses remain proposals until reviewed. Not every choice has to impose a tragedy.

Explicit warnings are reserved for the largest irreversible commitments (D49). Ordinary choices rely on understandable context and dialogue. Essential facts and the player's intended action should remain clear without revealing every future outcome. Exact warning thresholds, wording, save timing, and reload/reversal behavior remain open.

Future branch briefs should track consequences across missions, character participation, accessible routes, settlements, and endings as appropriate. Define what differs during play, not only in a final summary. Count mutually exclusive content separately when estimating per-run duration and production work.

In the accepting-power branch, Caedran demands protected witnesses whose testimony threatens his scheme (D128). Compliance, open refusal, and attempted deception are playable responses (D129). Compliance brings captivity/coercion with difficult later rescue possible (D130); refusal revokes offices and starts an arrest/seizure attempt (D131). The deception stages a prison-transfer escape (D132). Full success preserves witness safety and office/apparent loyalty (D133); exposure revokes office and starts a playable arrest/extraction, where witness escape remains possible (D134). Establish actual knowledge and protection through the trio's experience. At Hearthmere's giant defense, the trio publicly exposes Caedran's role to rally resistance. Serving insiders sacrifice their cover and offices in that act (D135, D138). Sera and her technicians are the witnesses (D136). Their optional prison rescue includes substantial puzzles, a warden boss, and a coordinated escape (D137, D139). Choosing a final campaign at C06 closes the rescue window (D140–D142). Before confirmation, identify Sera and the technicians who remain captive and state that they will die permanently if the player commits. Present the warning while the player can still attempt the dungeon. Required scenes use earned alternative expertise when Sera is absent. Exact evidence and rescue mechanics remain open. Rescue does not erase a handover or guarantee forgiveness; arrest/exposure is not an automatic cinematic loss.

## Cinematic viewpoint

The trio's first direct encounter with Caedran is a tense mid-campaign parley after they know he manufactures the attacks (D122). He wants dangerous research and evidence surrendered and offers positions of power (D123–D124). Acceptance/refusal is a consequential player choice (D125); acceptance grants genuine authority, followed by pressure to use it his way (D126). Show that authority changing actual operations and people's lives. The offices are military command for the warrior, royal research leadership for the mage, and intelligence leadership for the assassin, with inner-council seats and frontier policy authority for all three (D127). Exact titles, jurisdiction, council procedures, materials, duties, demanded orders, motives, staging, and enforcement remain proposals. Do not assume acceptance means a secret infiltration plan; it needs authored intent and follow-through. The established eventual-antagonist role remains unchanged.

**Confirmed (D121):** occasional cutscenes can show events the trio does not witness, including private Caedran/mother scenes. This permits dramatic irony and personal context while the gameplay protagonist stays fixed. Keep player knowledge and the heroes' knowledge distinct: a private confession is not automatically a journal discovery or evidence available in the commander confrontation.

Proposed interlude opportunities include a royal report after a consequential operation, Caedran's response to the giant's death, and a private exchange before a late strategic escalation. These are candidate placements, not three newly approved scenes or a mandatory cutaway after every mission. Each needs a concrete narrative change or revelation; avoid repeating the same insult/argument without development.

Private scenes must use the actual campaign facts. A dead officer cannot deliver a report, an escaped prisoner cannot appear back in captivity, and the mother's contempt does not automatically turn her into the source of every military order. Reveal only what serves the chosen suspense and avoid explaining a mystery before the player has a reason to care. Scene inventory and exact reveal timing remain open.

Apply the existing mostly 1–3-minute major-turn pacing, occasional longer scenes, and pause/skip support. Trigger interludes at coherent story transitions with clear return to the current adventure. They do not establish hidden world timers, new playable characters, or a separate replay library. Exact save/interruption behavior remains part of technical scene design.

## Playable action and cinematic scenes

The player controls one chosen hero throughout; the other two act independently with occasional player-triggered team attacks/finishers (D16, D19). Major bosses are mostly playable, with short cinematic phase transitions and spectacular finishers (D21).

Heroes begin grounded and develop superhuman but contained speed, skill, techniques, and power against gigantic enemies. Temporary awakened forms have distinct visuals and moves (D12, D18, D20). Cinematic feats must fit that ceiling rather than introducing world-shattering powers unrelated to playable progression.

Awakening potential originates in the shared opening catastrophe and grows through training, discoveries, and experience (D55). Each hero also has one signature puzzle utility: mage item teleportation, assassin stealth, and warrior spear-vaulting (D56). Use is broad during exploration, with tighter important-story rules, and puzzles span major dungeon challenges and smaller discoveries (D57–D59). The user's moat example establishes coordinated roles once an objective is clear: the player performs their selected hero's part, AI companions execute the others in sequence, and teamwork opens a shared route. Companions gradually offer hints when stuck (D60–D62). Exact rules, first-use scenes, failure, and recovery remain to be designed. Keep utilities distinct from temporary awakened forms unless a later decision links them. See the [exploration and puzzle brief](EXPLORATION_PUZZLES.md).

Awakening is manually activated after building a combat meter (D72). Its unlock and first-use scenes must respect that playable system while earning the dramatic moment. It temporarily replaces the normal build with a fixed class-specific awakened moveset (D79), then returns to the selected normal build. Exact attacks, charge/drain, duration, and AI activation remain open.

Awakened appearance is a major humanoid transformation, substantially changing hair, outfit/armor, and silhouette while preserving identity (D77). Equipped weapons and armor also visibly change the ordinary hero (D76). Four behavior-changing ability trees per class have successive layers of branching choices (D75, D78); the fixed awakened kit is separate from those normal branches. Scenes must eventually represent the selected hero's custom appearance and visible gear consistently rather than silently replacing them with an unrelated fixed model.

### Cinematic pacing

Substantial cinematic scenes mark major story turning points, while ordinary conversations stay brief and in-world (D80). This concentrates scene production around important changes rather than turning every interaction into an extended movie. Bosses stay mostly playable with short phase transitions and spectacular finishers (D21).

Most story cinematics target approximately 1–3 minutes, with occasional longer major turning points (D81). These are pacing targets rather than fixed quotas. Players can pause and skip scenes, without a replay library (D82). Skipping is not restricted to scenes already seen. Exact controls and interrupted-scene resume remain to be designed.

Candidate turning points include the crisis that brings the strangers together, earned first awakenings, consequential alliance decisions, major losses, and distinct endings. These are planning priorities; the exact scene inventory, lengths, and branch variants remain to be authored.

Exact shot lists, per-scene timing, camera grammar, finisher triggers, awakening staging, interactivity, skip state resolution, and mid-cinematic resume remain open. The prototype's fixed finisher duration is a reference, not a mandatory template. A proposed skip rule preserves unresolved player decisions rather than selecting their answer automatically. A replay library is outside the selected direction.

## Musical identity

Use an orchestral foundation, from intimate character themes to enormous boss scores, blended with electronic and rock elements for battles (D83). Do not turn this into an exclusively rock/metal-driven score or require every emotional scene to use the same battle instrumentation.

Recurring themes for characters, the trio's growing bond, resistance priorities, and Caedran can tie changing events together. These are proposed scoring roles, not approved melodies or a track inventory. Exact instrumentation, adaptive layers, production method, and audio delivery remain open.

## Mobile presentation context

The primary experience is browser-link play on Apple and Android phones/tablets. Desktop adapts. Ordinary play uses the original isometric camera, assisted targeting, and manual movement and combat. Minimum 60 fps on iPhone 14 Pro is a requirement to verify, not an achieved result (D24–D28, D153).

Players primarily spend 30–60 minutes per session and should be able to stop almost anywhere and resume the same situation (D29–D30). Cinematic interruption/resume rules still need detail. Compose scenes, text, gestures, and effects for phone viewing, and plan performance against the reference-device requirement.

Guest play, optional cloud/cross-device sync, one evolving save with recovery backups per hero, and downloaded-content offline play are confirmed (D84–D86). Narrative decisions persist in that timeline. Package required offline vocal/music content without depending on a live generation call for every scene; exact production and delivery remain open. See [the saving brief](SAVE_AND_OFFLINE.md).
